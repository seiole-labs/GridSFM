# Technical blog source audit

## Bottom line

The proposed story is defensible if it distinguishes **seen-topology scenario tests** from **zero-shot topology-and-scale tests**. The largest correction is citation scope: [arXiv:2605.04289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04289) documents the open-data grid-construction pipeline, not GridSFM's five scenario perturbations. Cite the [GridSFM white paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), section 5, for those perturbations.

## Claims that are supported

- **What GridSFM is.** GridSFM is a shared, graph-based, physics-informed neural surrogate for AC-OPF. Given topology, operating constraints, generator characteristics, and load, it predicts bus voltage magnitude and angle, active and reactive generator dispatch, and a feasibility score. Branch flows are then calculated analytically from the predicted bus state. It is not a conventional AC-OPF solver. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), sections 1, 3.2, and 4.)
- **Open release.** Microsoft released the roughly 15M-parameter GridSFM-Open tier under MIT, along with source code, a training pipeline, evaluation tooling, PowerModels.jl warm-start integration, and a checkpoint. A safe acknowledgement is: “We thank the GridSFM authors for releasing the model, checkpoint, and tooling; that release made independent experimentation possible.” ([Microsoft project page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/gridfm/); [GitHub repository](https://github.com/microsoft/GridSFM); white paper section 11.) Do not imply that the complete half-million-scenario training corpus was released unless separately verified.
- **Training corpus and ceiling.** The white paper reports about 200 base grids and more than 500,000 scenarios from PGLib-OPF, OPFData, and Microsoft's open US-grid corpus. For the released checkpoint, the exact reported largest training graph is `case4661_sdet`, with 4,661 buses—not merely “about 4,000” when calculating OOD scale factors. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), sections 5 and 7.)
- **Five perturbation axes.** Section 5 varies five axes “independently and in combination”: load profiles; generator availability/outages; line-rating derating; voltage-limit tightening; and generator merit-order/cost shuffling. The paper gives 30%, 20%, and 15% scenario probabilities for generator outages, line derating, and voltage tightening, respectively. Load is sampled at 0.8–1.5 times nominal with per-load jitter; costs are shuffled on 40% of generators per scenario. This supports “combined probabilistic perturbations,” but “all five perturbations are applied to every sample” would be false. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), section 5, pp. 4–5.)
- **Companion pipeline provenance.** arXiv:2605.04289 describes five *construction stages*: OpenStreetMap extraction, topology reconstruction, electrical-parameter estimation, demand allocation, and DC/AC-OPF solving. It produced models for 48 contiguous US states and six multi-state regions. It is appropriate to cite this paper for the provenance of the US-derived base grids, then cite the GridSFM white paper for scenario perturbations. ([Open-data paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04289), abstract and sections 1.2 and 3.)
- **PGLib provenance.** PGLib-OPF is an open-access benchmark library created with a standardized AC-OPF formulation to make algorithm comparisons more consistent. It combines cases from several families and fills missing OPF data using documented models. Describe these as benchmark networks, not categorically as faithful operational grids. ([PGLib-OPF paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02788), abstract and sections IV–VI; [official repository](https://github.com/power-grid-lib/pglib-opf).)
- **Zero-shot terminology.** “Zero-shot evaluation under topology and scale shift” is accurate when the released checkpoint is used without fitting, fine-tuning, or test-time adaptation on an unseen graph above 4,661 buses. The white paper itself uses “OOD, zero-shot” for `case6470_rte`, a 6,470-bus graph about 1.4 times its training maximum. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), section 7, Tables 7–8.)

## Red-team corrections

1. **Do not call the first suite a validation cut.** These are newly sampled scenarios on base topologies seen during pretraining, not necessarily Microsoft's literal validation split. Use **“held-out scenarios on seen base topologies,” “seen-topology test,”** or **“almost-ID.”** The model has topology-specific exposure, so this is not a clean unseen-grid test.

2. **Do not call PGLib source-family OOD.** PGLib-OPF and OPFData were part of pretraining. The defensible shift is in the exact topology and graph scale, while the task, file conventions, and benchmark family remain familiar. Prefer **“topology-and-scale OOD”** to “true,” “pure,” or “entirely unseen-distribution” OOD.

3. **The blanket “more than 2× training size” claim is wrong for the current accepted suite.** With a 4,661-bus maximum, `case6470_rte` is 1.39 times and `case8387_pegase` is 1.80 times; only the 13,659- and 20,758-bus accepted cases exceed 2 times. Say **“1.39–4.45 times the largest training graph, with two accepted cases above 2 times,”** or **“up to 4.45 times larger.”**

4. **`case6470_rte` is not a wholly new OOD target.** Microsoft's white paper already evaluates an OPFData version of that topology zero-shot, using both intact and N-1 splits. A vanilla PGLib operating point is a different scenario, but the blog should not imply that it is the first published GridSFM evaluation on this graph.

5. **“Tests the model to the limit” is promotional, not demonstrated.** Larger exploratory cases exist, and the accepted suite is small. Use **“a deliberately strict scale-extrapolation test”** or state the measured range.

6. **Do not say GridSFM has no physics constraints.** Training includes power-balance, thermal-limit, and voltage-bound penalties, and branch flows are analytically derived. The precise limitation is that inference does **not hard-project the complete output onto the AC-OPF feasible set**, so low average error does not guarantee KCL or limit satisfaction. Attribute observed violations to the absence of hard feasibility enforcement only as a plausible explanation, not a proven cause. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), sections 3–5 and 10.)

7. **Treat the feasibility score carefully.** A converged reference AC-OPF solution is evidence that a tested instance is feasible, so a low GridSFM feasibility probability on that instance is a false negative. But solver *non-convergence* does not generally prove mathematical infeasibility. The score predicts instance solvability/feasibility; it does not certify that GridSFM's raw predicted dispatch satisfies KCL and operating limits.

8. **Grid size is a hypothesis, not an isolated cause.** A probability drop correlated with bus count across a handful of different topologies cannot establish that size caused the drop. Say the pattern is **“consistent with scale sensitivity, confounded by topology and case family.”** The white paper similarly finds feasibility-classifier collapse on its 6,470-bus OOD probe, but does not isolate size experimentally.

9. **Keep latency claims within the timers used.** If GridSFM request time and fresh AC solve time have different boundaries, call them component timings, not a formal end-to-end speedup. Report hardware, process state, batching, warm/cold status, and repetitions where available.

10. **Warm-starting is established prior work, while the local extension can still be novel.** The white paper reports a 1.66× geometric-mean AC-OPF solver speedup from a GridSFM partial warm start over 54 grids, but performance varies by grid and that result concerns solver time under its setup. Frame ongoing work as an independent reproduction or broader OOD/hybrid evaluation—not as the first proposal to warm-start AC-OPF with GridSFM. ([White paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GridFM_white_paper.pdf), section 6.3 and Table 5.)

## Suggested compact framing

> We first evaluate newly sampled operating scenarios on base topologies represented during GridSFM pretraining. This is a seen-topology, validation-like stress test rather than a clean test of unseen-grid generalization. We then use the released checkpoint without adaptation on unperturbed PGLib-OPF networks ranging from 1.39 to 4.45 times the largest reported training graph. Because PGLib-derived data appeared in pretraining, we describe this specifically as zero-shot generalization under topology and scale shift—not source-family shift.
